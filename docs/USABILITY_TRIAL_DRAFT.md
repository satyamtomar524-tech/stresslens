# StressLens camera-off usability trial — draft

**Status:** Planning draft. No participants have been recruited and no trial has taken place. Review the local report and setup before inviting anyone. This is a usability exercise, not a test of stress detection, a clinical study or evidence of health benefits.

## Purpose and scope

Find out whether adults can set up the local prototype, use the work clock and voluntary check-ins, understand a session report and find its limitations. Use fictional entries for the task; real health information, camera images, videos and wearable readings are not needed. A single laptop or a local copy on a participant's own computer is sufficient for an initial pilot; there is currently no remotely hosted service or verified clean-machine setup.

## Before starting

1. Show the participant the current version and the camera-off interface. Explain that participation is voluntary, they can skip any step or question and stop without giving a reason, and their decision has no consequence.
2. Explain that work state can be lost on reload or process loss, reminders appear in the open app only, and the prototype does not diagnose or infer stress from a face. Keep camera/SDK consent and local history off. Use a disposable app session and fictional answers; do not use existing personal records.
3. Agree the data choice before observation. **Default: nothing is shared and no report is sent.** The participant may separately choose to tell the facilitator their feedback, share a written summary, or share an exported fictional report after reviewing its contents. They may decline each item. No screenshots, recordings or raw camera data are required. If someone volunteers any file, they choose what to share and can remove identifiers first. Do not collect a real report without a separate explicit choice.
4. Explain where any voluntarily shared feedback would be stored, who can read it, how long it will be kept, and how it can be removed. These operational details must be agreed before collecting feedback; do not promise deletion from copies you cannot control. Do not post or upload participant feedback without their separate permission.
5. Confirm opt-in using a simple question: “Are you comfortable trying the fictional, camera-off demo under these choices?” A verbal yes is sufficient for this informal pilot; record only that permission was given if they agree to notes. A no or uncertainty means do not begin.

## Suggested 10–15 minute journey

1. Start work, enter one fictional check-in, pause, resume and end.
2. Find the work-session report. Explain in their own words what the active time, chart and summary mean.
3. If they wish, try the explicit closing-check-in choice; observe whether it is clear that an unchecked answer stays standalone.
4. Optionally open the fictional report downloads and inspect them locally. Share a file only through the separate choice above. Do not enable history, camera, SDK, or real device imports.
5. Ask the questions below. Record only notes the participant agrees to share. Avoid coaching before the participant has had a chance to try each step.

## Feedback questions

1. What did you think StressLens was meant to help you do?
2. Which step was easy or confusing? Where did you expect to find the report?
3. What do you think the chart and short explanation describe? Was anything unclear or surprising?
4. What did you understand about the closing-check-in option and work time after End?
5. Would the optional break prompts feel useful, distracting or neither? Why?
6. What would you change first? Is there anything you would not want the app to collect?
7. Which feedback, if any, may be retained or shared, and for what purpose?

## Withdrawal and record

A participant can pause or stop at any time and skip questions. If they withdraw, stop collection and ask what they want done with any feedback they already shared. Delete or return local copies that are under the facilitator's control if requested; explain any copy already shared beyond that control. Participation does not authorize future contact or publication.

Keep a minimal private note only with permission: date, prototype version/commit, tasks attempted, observations and user-approved feedback. Avoid names, health details, photos and identifiers. Count participants accurately, distinguish observed actions from opinions and do not infer clinical accuracy or effectiveness. Summarize findings as usability observations from the actual small group; verify any change with fresh fictional tests.

## Outstanding decisions

Choose the facilitator, storage location, access, retention period and removal process before inviting people. Confirm the local install and camera-off browser journey, and prepare an informed explanation in language participants understand. Do not send invitations, configure cloud hosting or collect participant data as part of this draft.
